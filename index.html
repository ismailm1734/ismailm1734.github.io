Match Villains Economy Lab

Problem

How does a match-3 game balance its difficulty curve against in-app purchase revenue? How hard can you push a player before they quit instead of paying?

What I did

I played Match Villains to level 211, recording where difficulty spikes landed and what triggered purchase prompts, then built an interactive simulator in vanilla JavaScript around the levers a real economy designer actually turns: win rates on normal and wall levels, how often walls appear, the share of losses that end just short of the goal, the buy-rate at each of those moments, pack prices, and the lives gate. The model runs a cohort of 1,000 players through 120 levels and reports revenue per player, purchases, end retention, the difficulty curve, and where players drop off.

What I found

Difficulty walls arrive roughly every 15 levels. The game runs no ads at all — revenue is entirely IAP. The extra-moves offer is triggered on near-win losses, when the player runs out of moves just short of the goal. Sweeping wall difficulty through the model shows the core trade-off: harder walls raise revenue per player until churn overtakes the gain, so the balance point is a sweet spot rather than a straight line.
